How did OpenSearch store the data?

First it split the text into words: the analyzer

An analyzer is a filter that changes text to lowercase and splits it into words. The analyzer of Lale Market, tr_folded, does one more step and simplifies the Turkish letters: ı → i, ş → s, ğ → g, ü → u. So also if your keyboard has no Turkish letters, the search for kulaklik finds Kulaklık. In the dark box above, you can see the words that the two analyzers made from the same text, side by side. The words that differ have a color.

Then it kept a list for each word: the inverted index

When OpenSearch adds the products, it writes in advance in which products each word occurs. During a search, it does not read the 200 products one by one. It looks only at the lists of the words that you search for. The rows below come live from these lists, for the words of your query.

An analogy from daily life

  

    
    

      
You look for the recipes with eggplant in a thick cookbook. You do not turn the pages one by one. You look at the index at the back of the book:
        eggplant: pages 12, 48, 103. You go to three pages, and you are done.

      
The inverted index of OpenSearch is the same kind of index, with three differences: it accepts your typos, it finds words without Turkish letters,
        and it puts the result that matches best at the top.

{% block glossary %}


  
Terms on this page

  

    
Token
One word part that the analyzer takes from the text.

    
Analyzer
A filter that changes text to lowercase, splits it into words and simplifies them.

    
Inverted index
A list from each word to the documents that contain it.

    
Fuzziness
A setting that accepts a difference of a few letters in a word.

    
Relevance score (BM25)
A number that shows how well a result matches the query.

    
Aggregation
Group and count the results in the same query. The filter list comes from it.
